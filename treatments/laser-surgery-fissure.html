UNDERSTANDING ANAL FISSURE AND ITS COMMON SYMPTOMS

What Is an Anal Fissure?

An anal fissure is a small tear or crack in the delicate lining of the anus. It commonly causes sharp or burning pain during and after bowel movements and may sometimes lead to bright-red bleeding.

Anal fissures can develop due to hard or dry stools, constipation, straining during bowel movements, or repeated irritation of the anal area. In some cases, diarrhoea or increased muscle tightness around the anus may also contribute.

  • check_circleSahrp pain during or after bowel movements
  • check_circleBright-red blood on toilet paper or stool
  • check_circleBurning or irritation around the anus
  • check_circleDiscomfort while sitting
  • check_circleFear or difficulty passing stools because of pain
RECOGNISING THE COMMON SIGNS OF ANAL FISSURE

Common Symptoms of Anal Fissure

Fissure symptoms can vary from person to person. Common symptoms include pain, bleeding and discomfort around bowel movements.

  • Sharp Pain

    Pain during or after bowel movements.

  • Bright-Red Bleeding

    Small amounts of fresh blood may be noticed.

  • Burning

    Burning or irritation around the anus.

  • Muscle Spasm

    Tightness or spasm of the anal muscles.

  • Persistent Discomfort

    Discomfort that may continue after passing stool.

COMMON FACTORS THAT MAY CONTRIBUTE TO ANAL FISSURE

What Can Cause an Anal Fissure?

Anal fissures can develop for several reasons. Identifying contributing factors can help with treatment and prevention of recurrence.

  • check_circle

    Constipation Hard or infrequent stools can increase pressure on the anal lining.

  • check_circle

    Excessive Straining Repeated straining can increase stress on the area.

  • check_circle

    Local Trauma Certain forms of local injury may contribute to fissure formation.

  • check_circle

    Hard Stools Passing hard stools can cause stretching and injury.

  • check_circle

    Frequent Diarrhoea Repeated loose stools may irritate the anal lining.

  • check_circle

    Muscle Spasm Increased sphincter tension may interfere with healing.

UNDERSTANDING THE DIFFERENCES TO GUIDE THE RIGHT TREATMENT

Acute vs. Chronic Anal Fissure

Understanding the nature of the fissure helps determine the appropriate treatment approach.

Acute Fissure

A newer fissure that may heal with appropriate conservative care.

  • Bright-red blood on toilet paper or stool
  • Burning or irritation around the anus
  • Discomfort while sitting
  • Fear or difficulty passing stools because of pain

Chronic Fissure

A persistent or recurrent fissure that may require further treatment.

  • Persistent or recurring symptoms
  • May have associated muscle spasm
  • May not respond adequately to conservative care
  • May require procedural treatment
GENTLE, NON-SURGICAL APPROACHES TO SUPPORT HEALING

Conservative Treatment Options

Depending on the condition, non-surgical treatment may be recommended before considering a procedure.

  • 1

    Adequate Fluids

    Adequate hydration can help support softer bowel movements.

  • 2

    Fibre-Rich Diet

    Suitable dietary fibre can help improve bowel regularity.

  • 3

    Bowel Management

    Avoiding constipation and excessive straining can support healing.

  • 4

    Medicines

    Prescribed topical or oral medicines may be recommended when appropriate.

WHEN ADDITIONAL EVALUATION OR PROCEDURAL CARE MAY BE NEEDED

When Conservative Treatment May Not Be Enough

If symptoms continue despite appropriate conservative treatment, your doctor may recommend further evaluation and discuss procedural treatment options.

ACCURATE EVALUATION TO IDENTIFY THE RIGHT CONDITION

A Proper Diagnosis Comes First

Anal pain and bleeding can have several causes. A detailed evaluation helps distinguish fissure from other anorectal conditions and guides treatment selection.

  • check_circleClinical examination
  • check_circleAssessment of fissure characteristics
  • check_circleReview of previous treatments

WHEN LASER TREATMENT MAY BE APPROPRIATE

Who May Be Suitable for Laser Treatment?

Laser treatment is not necessary for every fissure. Suitability is determined after clinical assessment.

Laser Treatment May Be Considered For

  • Persistent or chronic fissure symptoms
  • Selected patients with ongoing pain
  • Recurrent fissure symptoms
  • Patients who have not improved adequately with conservative care
  • Patients assessed as suitable by the surgeon

Treatment May Differ When

  • The diagnosis is uncertain
  • Symptoms suggest another anorectal condition
  • Additional medical conditions affect treatment choice
  • Another procedure is clinically more appropriate
  • The patient's individual anatomy requires another approach
