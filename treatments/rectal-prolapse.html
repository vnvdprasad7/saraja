About Perianal Abscess

What Is Rectal Prolapse?

Rectal prolapse is a condition in which the rectum loses its normal support and slips downward, sometimes protruding through the anal opening. It may initially occur during bowel movements or straining and return inside on its own, but repeated episodes can become more frequent and persistent over time.

It may be associated with constipation, difficulty passing stool, mucus discharge, rectal discomfort, bleeding, or a feeling of incomplete bowel emptying. Early evaluation can help identify the severity of the prolapse and guide appropriate treatment and supportive care.

RECOGNISE THE WARNING SIGNS

Symptoms of Rectal Prolapse

Recognising the symptoms early can help identify rectal prolapse and guide timely evaluation. Symptoms may vary depending on the severity and type of prolapse.

  • Bulging Tissue
  • Rectal Discomfort
  • Mucus Discharge
  • Rectal Bleeding
  • Constipation
  • Straining During Bowel Movements
  • Bowel Leakage
  • Feeling of Incomplete Emptying
KNOW WHAT INCREASES THE RISK

Causes & Risk Factors

Understanding the factors that may weaken pelvic support and increase the risk of rectal prolapse.

Chronic Constipation

Repeated straining during bowel movements may contribute to pelvic support problems.

Weak Pelvic Floor

Reduced support from pelvic muscles and surrounding tissues may contribute to prolapse.

Age-Related Changes

Pelvic muscles and supporting tissues may weaken with age.

Previous Childbirth

Vaginal childbirth can contribute to pelvic floor weakening.

Chronic Diarrhoea

Repeated bowel disturbances may increase the risk of rectal prolapse.

Heavy Straining or Lifting

Repeated pressure can place additional stress on pelvic support structures.

UNDERSTAND THE DIFFERENT TYPES

Types of Rectal Prolapse

Explore the different types of rectal prolapse and understand how each type affects the rectum.

Internal Rectal Prolapse

The rectum slides downward inside the pelvis without protruding through the anus.

Partial / Mucosal Prolapse

Only the inner lining of the rectum protrudes through the anus.

Full-Thickness Prolapse

The full thickness of the rectal wall protrudes through the anus.

Diagnosis & Evaluation

Accurate evaluation helps understand the severity of rectal prolapse and identify the most appropriate treatment approach.

  • Clinical Examination

    Assessment of the prolapse, anal sphincter, and surrounding structures.

  • Defecography

    Helps assess rectal movement and pelvic-floor function.

  • Anorectal Manometry

    Evaluates anal and rectal muscle function.
